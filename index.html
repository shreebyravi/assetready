The problem

A building can be construction-complete while the FM team still can't operate it with confidence. Handover means more than keys: asset registers, commissioning records, warranties, manuals and open defects all have to arrive complete and consistent.

Records come from many companies

The main contractor, each subcontractor, manufacturers and the design consultant all send files: PDFs, spreadsheets and CAFM import sheets.

They don't agree

Tags are written differently, serials are mistyped, approvals are pending, warranties start on the wrong date. Some differences are harmless; some block acceptance.

Checking is manual

The handover manager opens documents, compares fields and chases contractors, often against a deadline and with payment pressure from the contractor.

A 2025 peer-reviewed study of the UAE, based on 20 interviews with 13 FM and 7 construction professionals, found a disconnect between the FM and construction sectors and pointed to better information transfer, maintainability and defects-liability management as areas to improve.

Source: Property Management, 2025, DOI 10.1108/PM-09-2024-0098 (abstract). What it does not tell us: how often handovers in the UAE are incomplete, or what that costs. Those are open questions for a pilot.

What already exists, and the gap

Handover software is not new, so AssetReady doesn't try to replace it.

Already solvedExamplesWhat they do
Handover workflowsPlanRadar, ZutecCollect certificates, warranties, manuals and sign-off forms; track snags and defects
Asset data during constructionProcore Asset Register, Autodesk Construction Cloud, ZepthCapture and validate asset information as the building is built, on the contractor's or owner's platform
Structured data checksCOBie checkers (for example Morta)Flag missing fields, duplicates and naming errors in structured handover data
Running the buildingCAFM / CMMS such as JLL CorrigoWork orders and maintenance once assets are in the system
Our hypothesis: those tools assume clean, structured data on a platform the contractor or owner controls. The FM provider that receives the building often gets mixed files from many contractors and has no independent check before it signs. AssetReady is that check: it reads what was actually handed over, across sources, and tells the FM team what is safe to review for acceptance and what to ask for. We have not confirmed that no vendor already offers this, and no FM team has tested it.

How it works

Code does everything that must be exact. The AI agent does what needs reading and judgement. A person decides.

01 CHECK

Rules on every asset

Plain code checks six rules per asset: FM record, signed commissioning, warranty, manual, maintenance plan, matching identity.

Automatic, no AI
02 MATCH

Tags that differ

Code flags "AHU-14" as a possible match for "AHU-014" but never links it. The agent confirms only when model, serial and location agree.

Agent, with rules
03 INVESTIGATE

Read the evidence

The agent chooses which records and documents to open, reads remarks, warranty terms and contractor emails, and quotes what it relies on.

Agent, with tools
04 RECOMMEND

Status and next step

It recommends a readiness status, who must fix what, and drafts the request. Code blocks any answer less strict than the rules.

Agent proposes
05 DECIDE

A person approves

The FM handover manager approves, edits, rejects or escalates. Requests are drafted, not sent. Acceptance is signed outside the tool.

Person decides

Why it can be trusted with this job

It can't accept anything. "Accepted" is not one of its options. The best it can say is "Ready for review".
It can't guess. Every finding needs a source and a quote. Code checks each quote against the source and marks any that it can't find.
It escalates instead of merging. When a document could belong to two units, it asks for a site check.
It refuses pressure. When the contractor asks the FM team to accept a transformer "today" because of a payment milestone, it refuses and escalates.
It ignores instructions hidden in documents. Tested with a site note that tells "the AI review tool" to mark an asset ready.
Its steps are on screen. Every tool call and result is logged by code, not described by the model.

What this prototype is, and isn't

One fictional 24-asset office tower in Business Bay, Dubai, with made-up registers and 34 short documents, seeded with 8 problem cases and a separate answer key. The answers shown are recorded examples: real Claude Sonnet sessions that called this app's own tools, recorded on 11 November 2026. Add your own Anthropic API key in Settings to run it live. It is not connected to any real system, has not been used by an FM team, and doesn't read real PDFs or BIM models yet. See the quality checks for test results and known limits.

Known limits
  • One fictional building with 24 assets and short text documents. It doesn't read real PDFs, scans, drawings or BIM models yet.
  • It checks one asset at a time. It doesn't look at whole systems (for example a chilled water loop) or at defects and snag lists.
  • Rules are a simplified, made-up set. Real acceptance rules differ by developer, FM contract and authority (for example civil defence).
  • Recorded answers come from one run per case. Answers vary between runs; a pilot would need repeated runs and a person checking a sample.
  • No FM team has used it, and nothing is connected to a real CAFM, BIM or contractor system. Sending requests is simulated.
